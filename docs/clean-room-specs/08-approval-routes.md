# 08. Approval routes

Spec `spec-approval-routes-001` · compatibility: behavior-compatible · leakage review: passed

## Summary

Configurable approval routes: a route is a template bound to one record type (project-specific or tenant-wide) with ordered steps; each step names either a role or one user, an aggregation mode, an optional required approver count, an optional time budget and an optional amount band. Starting a route against a record creates a workflow instance that moves step by step; each decision is approve or reject (there is no separate sign-off concept), one rejection ends the workflow, and clearing the last applicable step approves it. Deciders may change by one-tap reassignment or by out-of-office delegation. A periodic background sweep nudges the holder of a step that overran its time budget and, after a grace window, escalates up the chain of later named approvers. A dry-run simulator, a per-instance timeline and project analytics help route authors and managers. A small set of read-only starter routes may be provided for review flows. All timing constants, caps, windows and numeric bounds in this spec are illustrative parameters; the destination chooses its own values.

## Behaviour

- A route has a name, one record type, an active flag, an optional project (absent means tenant-wide) and a bounded number of ordered steps (at least one; illustratively up to twenty). The record type is drawn from a configurable set of record types that the destination decides to support; the set is open-ended and is not fixed by this spec. A record type outside the configured set is a validation error. _(apr-beh-001; observed)_
- Step ordinals must be dense and start at 1 without gaps or duplicates. Each step names exactly one approver, either a role or one specific user (neither or both is a validation error). A step has an aggregation mode of all, any or majority (default all), an optional required approver count (bounded, illustratively 1 to 100) that declares the size of the eligible population for role steps, an optional time budget in hours (bounded, illustratively 1 to 720) and an optional amount band (minimum at least zero, maximum above zero), meaning the step takes part in routing an amount when minimum is not above the amount and the amount is below the maximum. A step with no band applies to every amount; a missing maximum means no ceiling. _(apr-beh-002; observed)_
- Saving a route is refused with a validation error when a step names a single user and also sets a required approver count above one (one person can give only one approval, so the count can never be reached). Amount bands are checked together: a band whose minimum is not below its maximum, an amount range between bands, or an amount range below the first band when no step is unbanded, is an error that would let money through unsigned and is refused with a coded error listing the findings; two bounded bands claiming the same amounts is only a warning and the route saves. Open-ended top bands (a senior approver also signs above a threshold) are not overlaps. _(apr-beh-003; observed)_
- Route lifecycle rules: editing name or active flag is always allowed; replacing the step list is refused with a conflict once any instance exists on the route (so decision history is not lost); deleting a route is refused with a conflict once any instance references it (deactivate instead); a platform starter route cannot be edited or deleted (conflict) but can be cloned into a project, producing an ordinary editable active project route with the same steps (a starter or route with no steps cannot be cloned). Every route change is written to the audit trail. _(apr-beh-004; observed)_
- A small set of tenant-wide, read-only starter routes for a review record type may be provided and created idempotently at startup. Illustrative shapes: a single reviewer step in a lower-ranked role (mode all, count one, budget of a couple of days); a single approver step in a higher-ranked role (mode any, budget of a few days); and a two-stage route with a lower-ranked reviewer step followed by a higher-ranked authoriser step (each mode all, count one). The exact names, roles and budgets are the destination's choice. Each starter route ends in approved when simulated. _(apr-beh-005; observed)_
- Starting a workflow on a record requires an active route whose record type matches the request (otherwise validation errors), with at least one step. The routed amount decides which banded steps apply: for a purchase order it is read from the order itself (the net, pre-tax commitment and its currency) so the starter cannot route a large order past an approver by typing a smaller number; for other record types it is taken from the request with an optional currency; with no amount every step applies (missing information routes to more approvers, never fewer). If no step covers the amount the start is refused with a message to widen a band or add a step. Steps that do not apply are skipped for that instance. Only one pending workflow may exist per record (conflict otherwise). The instance begins at the first applicable step, records who started it and the amount, and writes an audit entry listing applicable and skipped step ordinals. _(apr-beh-006; observed)_
- Multi-step routes per record type: routes are matched to a record by type, so different record types carry different step chains; within a route steps run strictly in ordinal order, only the current step accepts decisions, and a decision on any other step is refused. Instance states are pending, approved, rejected and cancelled; step decisions are approved or rejected, each with an optional comment. A step's approver is a role (any user holding that role or a higher one) or a named user. The only difference between roles on a chain is who may decide: a chain can model a reviewer step followed by an approver step, but the engine has no distinct sign-off action, so reviewing and approving are both the same approve decision made at different steps. _(apr-beh-007; observed)_
- Deciding a step: the instance must be pending (conflict otherwise), the step must belong to the instance's route (validation error) and be the current step (conflict). Who may decide: if the step was reassigned, only that stand-in; else if the step names a user, that user or the terminal stand-in of their active delegation chain; else (role step) the caller's role must equal the required role or rank at or above it in the role hierarchy, with any role aliases normalized first, and unknown roles fail closed. Anyone else receives a permission-denied result. Each approver may record only one decision per step (conflict on a repeat). For record types configured with starter segregation, the person who started the workflow may reject or cancel but not approve (permission-denied); by default no record type has this restriction unless configured. _(apr-beh-008; observed)_
- Outcome of a decision: a rejection immediately ends the whole workflow as rejected, stamps completion time and clears any reassignment. An approval is judged against the step's clearing rule: for a step with a concrete decider set (named user, delegation or reassignment) that decider's approval clears it; mode any is cleared by the first approval; mode majority with a declared count is cleared when distinct approvers times two exceeds the count and there is no rejection, without a declared count when at least two decisions exist and approvals are more than half; mode all with a declared count is cleared when distinct approvers reach the count with no rejection, without a count it needs at least two distinct approvers so one sign-off cannot close a gate meant for several. A cleared step moves the workflow to the next applicable step by ordinal (clearing any per-step reassignment); clearing the last applicable step completes the workflow as approved. Every decision is audited with actor, previous and new state and comment, and lifecycle notifications (started, advanced, completed, rejected, cancelled) are published for other capabilities to act on, carrying the deciding user and comment. _(apr-beh-009; observed)_
- Decision concurrency: the instance is locked while a decision is processed so two approvers acting together are serialised and the second sees the post-advance state; a duplicate decision by one approver on one step collides with a uniqueness guarantee and is reported as a conflict. _(apr-beh-010; observed)_
- Cancelling a pending instance (manager-level permission) sets it cancelled with completion time and optional reason, audits it and publishes a cancellation notification; a non-pending instance cannot be cancelled (conflict). _(apr-beh-011; observed)_
- One-tap reassignment of the current step: only a user entitled to decide the step now, or a holder of the management capability, may hand it off (otherwise permission-denied); for a role step the actor's own role must satisfy the role. The workflow must be pending (conflict). The step must be clearable by one approval, otherwise the hand-off is refused with a validation error stating how many approvals the step needs (pinning one stand-in would weaken a multi-approver gate). The stand-in may not be the person who started the workflow, and must have access to the route's project when the route is project-specific (validation errors). The stand-in becomes the sole eligible decider for that step only, an in-app notification is sent to them, the hand-off is audited with previous and new assignee and reason, and a timeline notification is published. Reassignment is cleared when the step advances or the workflow ends. _(apr-beh-012; observed)_
- Delegation (out-of-office hand-off): a user hands their approvals to another user, optionally scoped to one project and optionally limited to a start and end time, with a reason. Delegating to oneself is refused; an unknown delegate or project is refused (validation error); creating requires an authenticated caller. Revoking is a soft deactivation that keeps history. While active, any step naming the delegator may also be decided by the delegator's terminal stand-in: chains are followed (A to B to C makes C the stand-in), the original approver remains eligible, intermediate people in a chain are not, cycles stop at the last safe person, and chains are cut after a small bounded number of links (illustratively five). A project-specific delegation beats a blanket one for the same delegator; a project-specific delegation does not apply when no project is known. A delegation applies lazily at decision time, so one created after the step began still works. _(apr-beh-013; observed)_
- Dry-run simulation of a route needs no running workflow: for each step it reports the fewest approvals that would clear it (named user one; any one; majority half the declared count rounded down plus one, or two when undeclared; all the declared count, or two when undeclared), a plain-language note and a flag when the step implicitly needs several approvers; it always walks a happy path (every step gets exactly what it needs) and optionally a what-if walk from supplied per-step approval, rejection and distinct-approver tallies, ending in completed, rejected (a rejection anywhere stops it) or stuck (a step that cannot clear, with its ordinal) and a trace. Warnings are given for role steps in all or majority mode with no declared count, for named approvers with a count above one, and for majority with count one behaving like any. The simulator and the running engine share one clearing rule so they cannot disagree. _(apr-beh-014; observed)_
- Time budget of a step: the due time is the moment the step became active plus its budget in hours; with no budget there is no deadline and the standing is on track. Standing is on track when the deadline is further away than a due-soon window (illustratively the final day), due soon within that window, breached at or after the deadline, with hours overdue or remaining reported. The moment a step became active is not stored, so it is reconstructed: for the first step it is the workflow start; for later steps it is the latest decision recorded on the step with the previous ordinal, falling back to the workflow start when none. All times are treated as UTC (naive values assumed UTC). A reminder text line is available in four forms conveying: overdue by some hours with due time, due in some hours with due time, no deadline, on track with due time. Wording is the destination's own. _(apr-beh-015; observed)_
- Time-budget monitor: a periodic background sweep (illustratively every half hour) runs inside the application and never raises out. It looks at pending workflows whose current step has a budget, and for each breached step it sends an in-app reminder with the instance, step and hours overdue to the step holder and publishes an overdue notification for the project timeline. The holder is, in order: the one-tap reassignment stand-in, else the named approver resolved through any active delegation, else the person who started the workflow; for a role step with no override and no starter the reminder has no recipient. A given step is reminded at most once per reminder spacing (illustratively about a day) and at most a small capped number of times in total (illustratively three); advancing to the next step starts a fresh count. One failing instance is logged and skipped without stalling the sweep. _(apr-beh-016; observed)_
- Escalation: after a step is breached, the same sweep evaluates escalation (even when the reminder was suppressed). The grace window is a configurable multiple of the step's budget (illustratively twice the budget) measured from when the step became active, so escalation happens once the step has been held for at least that long (inclusive). The escalation chain is the named users of the route's later steps in order; role-only steps contribute nobody, and anyone already entitled to decide the current step (the named approver, their delegate, or the reassigned stand-in) is excluded. The next target is the first chain entry not yet escalated to for this step and not equal to the current holder; the level is the number already escalated to plus one. When due, the target receives an in-app escalation notification (level, step, severity) and an escalation notification is published for the timeline; each target is escalated to at most once per step, remembered through earlier escalation notifications, and a new step starts a new ladder. When every entry is used or skipped, or the chain is empty, nothing happens and the stated reason is that no targets remain; before the window the stated reason is that the step is still within the window. _(apr-beh-017; observed)_
- Severity of an overrun for escalation display: hours overdue are held time minus budget floored at zero; zero is on time; up to a fraction of the budget late (illustratively half); up to a full budget breached; beyond that critical; a non-positive budget makes any overrun critical. A read-only escalation standing per instance reports whether it has a clock (false when not pending, no current step or no budget, then on time and no escalation), severity, hours overdue, whether to escalate, next target, level, reason, chain length and current holder, and uses the same entitlement and holder rules as the decision gate and the reminders so they cannot disagree. Severity thresholds are illustrative. _(apr-beh-018; observed)_
- Per-instance timeline: held time per step (each step is decided at its latest decision), total cycle time and time-budget breaches. Project analytics aggregate the project's own routes (not tenant-wide starter routes) over a rolling window (illustratively about six months) or an explicit start range (an inverted range is a validation error): exact status counts (total, pending, approved, rejected, cancelled) from the full set, other measures from a sample capped at a bounded size (illustratively a few thousand instances) with a truncated flag, grouped by approver role and by step, with a list of bottlenecks. A project with no routes returns an empty result. _(apr-beh-019; observed)_
- Other capabilities consume the workflow rather than the engine knowing them: a purchase order is read for its amount; record-owning capabilities outside this unit start instances and react to the terminal notifications; for several of them a pending or rejected instance blocks a downstream release. These consumers are outside this unit. _(apr-beh-020; inferred)_

## Inputs

- Route input: name, record type, optional project, active flag, steps (ordinal, role or user, mode, count, hours, amount band). Instance start input: route, record type, record reference, optional amount and currency. Decision input: step reference, approved or rejected, optional comment. Reassignment input: stand-in user, optional reason. Delegation input: delegate, optional project, optional start and end, optional reason. Simulation input: optional per-step tallies. Analytics input: record type filter, window or range. _(apr-in-001; observed)_

## Outputs

- Outputs: routes with their steps and a starter-route flag; instances with state, current step, started and completed times, amount and decision rows; simulation response; timeline; escalation standing; analytics; notifications. Money amounts keep two decimals. _(apr-out-001; observed)_

## States and transitions

- Instance: pending to approved (last applicable step cleared), to rejected (any rejection) or to cancelled (manual); approved, rejected and cancelled are final. Within pending, the current step number only increases, jumping over steps whose band excludes the amount. _(apr-st-001; observed)_

## Timing and ordering

- Illustrative timing parameters, all chosen by the destination: sweep cadence on the order of half an hour; reminder spacing on the order of a day with a small cap per step; due-soon window on the order of a day; escalation grace equal to a configurable multiple of the step budget; steps ordered by ordinal; decision serialisation per instance; lifecycle notifications are published detached so subscriber failures never break the transition. _(apr-time-001; observed)_

## Edge cases

- A role step in mode all or majority with no declared count needs at least two distinct approvers, a deliberate non-deadlocking default; a role step cannot be expanded to its members, so entitlement there is a role check on the caller. _(apr-edge-001; observed)_
- A reassignment or delegation moves the decider set and the clearing rule together, so an approval accepted from a stand-in always counts toward clearing the step. _(apr-edge-002; observed)_
- When band-skipped steps precede the current step, the reconstructed start of the current step uses the previous ordinal, which may be a skipped step with no decisions, so the time-budget clock then runs from the workflow start. Whether to keep this is a decision point. _(apr-edge-003; derived)_
- The last step of a route and any route whose later steps are role-only have an empty escalation chain, so they never escalate; their only follow-up is the capped reminders. _(apr-edge-004; derived)_

## Errors

- Result classes: unknown route, instance, delegation: not found; invalid ordinals, approver choice, record type, mode, count, bands: validation error; instance not pending, step not current, duplicate pending workflow, route has instances, starter-route edit, duplicate decision: conflict; not the assigned or named approver, role insufficient, starter approving a record type configured for starter segregation, reassignment by an unentitled user: permission-denied; reassignment to starter, to a single stand-in for a multi-approver step or to a user without project access, delegation to self: validation error; missing authentication on delegation creation: unauthorized. _(apr-err-001; observed)_

## What it must not do

- Nothing in the engine lets a decision change a step other than the current one, lets a rejection be overridden, lets a multi-approver step be satisfied by a single stand-in, or lets a delegation widen from one project to all projects by accident. _(apr-neg-001; observed)_

## Security

- Capabilities (illustrative tiers): read (lowest tier), write (contributor tier: create and edit routes, start workflows), decide (contributor tier), manage (manager tier: delete routes, cancel instances, reassign on behalf of others). Deciding additionally requires being entitled for that step, so holding the decide capability alone is not enough; a role step is gated by the caller's actual role so a lower-ranked user cannot clear a higher-ranked step. Segregation of duties for the starter is configured per record type and applies to none by default, so enabling it for a type is a deliberate decision. Reassignment targets are checked for project access and exclude the starter. All route, instance and delegation changes are audited. _(apr-sec-001; observed)_

## Invariants

- At most one pending workflow exists per record; a workflow's current step is always an applicable step for its amount; the simulator and the live engine give the same clearing answer for the same tallies. _(apr-inv-001; derived)_

## Notes

- Equal-output expectation: instance states pending, approved, rejected, cancelled; decisions approved or rejected; step modes all, any, majority; simulation outcomes completed, rejected, stuck; these generic lifecycle terms are the vocabulary consumers branch on. Escalation reasons (not yet in window, escalate, no targets remaining), overrun severity levels (on time, late, breached, critical) and time-budget standings (on track, due soon, breached) are distinct categories; exact wording is the destination's own. All numeric timing and bound values are illustrative parameters. _(apr-comp-001; observed)_

## Non-goals

- Consumer capabilities that start or react to workflows are out of scope.
- Localized message catalogues and notification wording are not reproduced.
- Interface component layout is not specified.
- Do not reproduce origin provenance markers.

## Test cases

- **apr-test-001**: Given a route with steps 1 (lower-ranked role, mode any) and 3 saved with ordinals 1 and 3, then it is refused; with ordinals 1 and 2 it is accepted.  
  Expected: First: validation error about dense ordinals. Second: saved.
- **apr-test-002**: Given a step naming one user and a required approver count of 3, when the route is saved, then it is refused.  
  Expected: Validation error suggesting a role step with that count instead.
- **apr-test-003**: Given bands 0 to 10000 and 15000 and above with nothing between, when saved, then refused; with 0 to 10000, 10000 to 50000 and 50000 and above, saved; with two bounded bands overlapping, saved with a warning.  
  Expected: Gap refused with coded findings; contiguous accepted; overlap accepted with warning.
- **apr-test-004**: Given a route with steps banded 0 to 10000 and 10000 and above (the second only for large amounts) and an unbanded final step, when a workflow starts with amount 4000, then the second step is skipped.  
  Expected: Workflow starts at step 1; after step 1 clears it moves to step 3; the audit entry lists step 2 as skipped.
- **apr-test-005**: Given a pending workflow on a purchase order, when a second start is attempted on the same order, or when the starter supplies a smaller amount than the order's net total, then the second start is refused and the order's own amount is used.  
  Expected: Conflict for duplicate; amount equals the order's net total.
- **apr-test-006**: Given a role step requiring a higher-ranked role, when a lower-ranked user tries to approve, then it is refused; when a user of the required role approves it clears.  
  Expected: Permission-denied for the lower-ranked user; step cleared for the qualifying user.
- **apr-test-007**: Given a role step in mode all with declared count 3, when two distinct approvers approve, then it stays pending; after the third it clears; with no declared count, one approval leaves it pending and two clear it; any rejection ends the workflow.  
  Expected: Clearing as described; rejected state final.
- **apr-test-008**: Given mode majority with count 5, when 2 then 3 distinct approvers approve, then the step clears at the third; with a rejection among them it never clears.  
  Expected: Cleared when distinct approvers times two exceeds five and no rejection exists.
- **apr-test-009**: Given an approver who already approved a step, when they approve again, then refused; given an old step, refused as not current.  
  Expected: Conflict results.
- **apr-test-010**: Given a record type configured for starter segregation, with a workflow started by Alice who also holds the required role, when Alice approves, then refused; Alice can reject or cancel.  
  Expected: Permission-denied for approval only.
- **apr-test-011**: Given a step naming Alice with Alice delegating to Bob and Bob delegating to Carol, all active, when each tries to decide, then Alice and Carol may, Bob may not.  
  Expected: Alice and Carol accepted; Bob permission-denied.
- **apr-test-012**: Given delegations Alice to Bob and Bob to Alice, when resolving Alice's stand-in, then the walk stops at the last safe person without error; delegating to oneself is refused; a project-limited delegation does not apply to another project.  
  Expected: No loop; validation error for self; not applied outside its project.
- **apr-test-013**: Given a pending step needing one approval, when its current approver reassigns it to Dana (with project access), then only Dana may decide, and a step needing two approvals refuses reassignment; reassigning to the starter is refused.  
  Expected: Dana sole decider with a notification; refusals as stated; override cleared on advance.
- **apr-test-014**: Under illustrative parameters (reminder spacing 20 hours, cap 3) and given a step with a 10 hour budget that became active 11 hours ago, when the sweep runs, then a reminder is sent to the holder; when it runs again an hour later, none; after the spacing another; after the cap is reached none more until the step advances.  
  Expected: Reminders respect the configured spacing and the per-step cap.
- **apr-test-015**: Under an illustrative grace multiple of two and given a route whose step 1 has a 10 hour budget and step 2 names user Erin, when step 1 has been held 19 hours, then no escalation; at 20 hours Erin is notified at level 1; at 30 hours nothing more (Erin already used, no targets remain).  
  Expected: Escalation exactly at the grace window, once per target.
- **apr-test-016**: Under the illustrative severity thresholds and given a step held for 3 hours past a 10 hour budget, then severity late; 8 hours past, breached; 11 hours past, critical; held within budget, on time.  
  Expected: Severity categories at ratios up to half, up to one, above one of the budget.
- **apr-test-017**: Given a route with an in-flight instance, when its steps are replaced, then refused; when the route is deleted, refused; deactivating works; a starter route cannot be edited but can be cloned.  
  Expected: Conflicts for replace and delete; clone produces an editable project route.
- **apr-test-018**: Given a route with a role step in mode all and no count, when simulated, then the step reports a minimum of two approvals and a warning; a what-if with a rejection on step 1 ends rejected; with one approval on that step ends stuck at step 1.  
  Expected: Outcomes happy path completed, scenario rejected or stuck with trace.
- **apr-test-019**: Given the starter routes provided by the destination, when each is simulated, then each completes as approved.  
  Expected: Happy path completed for all starter routes.

## Open questions (decisions for QC Manager)

- Decision point: escalation targets are only named users of later steps, so the last step and role-only chains never escalate. Decide whether the destination should also escalate to a role, to the route owner or to a project-level fallback.
- Decision point: the time-budget clock is reconstructed rather than stored and may start from the workflow start when preceding ordinals were skipped by amount bands. Decide whether to store a per-step activation time.
- Decision point: the escalation grace multiple and the reminder cap are fixed values; decide whether they should be fixed, per route or per record type.
- Decision point: de-duplication of reminders and escalations relies on previously sent notifications, so deleting those notifications would re-trigger them. A dedicated record is a design choice.
- Decision point: starter segregation applies to no record type by default. Decide per record type which should forbid self-approval (for example purchase orders, invoices, change orders).
- Decision point: there is no separate sign-off versus approve action; the engine has one approve decision and one reject decision, with reviewer and approver distinguished only by step order and role. Confirm this is the intended model for the destination.
- Decision point: role steps are gated by role rank, not by group membership, and a role step cannot be expanded to members, so counts for role steps are declared by the author rather than derived. Decide whether the destination can derive the eligible population.
- Decision point: when starting a workflow for a purchase order, a smaller amount supplied by the caller is silently ignored in favor of the order's own amount. Decide whether to ignore or reject.
- Decision point: which record types the destination supports and which of them carry a monetary amount is a destination configuration decision.
- Unknown: the aggregation internals of the timeline and analytics helpers (bottleneck definition, per-role and per-step statistics) were not analysed in detail.
- Unknown: interface behavior of the route editor, instance card, delegation manager, reassign dialog, escalation notice, simulation panel and analytics panel was only named and is not specified here.
- Unknown: how consumers react to terminal notifications for each record type.
